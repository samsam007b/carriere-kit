# Long-term strategy: vision, positioning, portfolio thinking

A single lead, taken or dropped, is a small event. The point of this file is to hold the
frame that turns a series of small events into a coherent trajectory, so the kit doesn't just
track applications, it supports a direction.

## Vision is a living document, not a five-year plan

`workspace/vision.md` records how decisions actually get made, not how they're supposed to be
made. If the person running the kit works by interest and exploration rather than a fixed
target, that belongs in the file explicitly: an AI assistant left to its own defaults will tend
to push toward a single locked-in objective (see PATTE-CAR-07 in
[05-career-anti-patterns.md](05-career-anti-patterns.md)), and a written note is what corrects
for that.

A target role can legitimately be "not fixed." When it isn't, the file should still state the
criteria that make a lead worth pursuing or not, since "not fixed" is not the same as "no
criteria."

Record settled trade-offs with a date and the basis for the decision. A trade-off can be
revisited later, but consciously, by writing what changed, not by relitigating it every few
weeks.

## Positioning: what the market says about the situation

`workspace/positioning.md` applies signaling theory and career capital theory to the current
situation, on a periodic basis (every few months, or when something material changes), not as a
one-time exercise.

Three questions it should keep answering:

1. **What's the signal?** In a market with asymmetric information, a signal shared by every
   other candidate doesn't distinguish anyone. What distinguishes a profile is a signal that's
   costly to fake: shipped work, checkable figures, results traceable by a third party. A
   degree supports institutional credibility; it doesn't lead.
2. **What capital is being built, and is it rare relative to a specific reference population?**
   A skill is only rare relative to a defined group; state which group. Distinguish an
   "auction" market (multiple buyers value different kinds of capital) from a "winner-take-all"
   one (one kind of capital dominates); the distinction changes whether keeping several leads
   open is a rational default or a stall.
3. **Where does that capital meet real demand?** Prefer structural signals (regulation,
   demographics, an irreversible shift) over cyclical trends, and note what cuts the other way
   too. A positioning note that lists only favorable signals isn't a positioning note.

## Portfolio thinking

Running several leads in parallel, including a mix across employment types (a role, a
freelance line, a personal project), is a viable state, not a failure to decide. The final
table described in
[03-follow-up-and-gates.md](03-follow-up-and-gates.md#the-final-table) exists partly to make a
portfolio visible: several qualified leads side by side, with the choice left to the person
running the kit.

Before treating two paths as mutually exclusive (an employment status and a side activity, for
instance), check whether the applicable legal framework actually allows combining them:
thresholds, contributions, disclosure obligations to an employer. Many choices that look binary
at first aren't. Record what was checked, with a source and a date, rather than assuming either
way.

## Known deadlines override strategy debates

Non-recoverable administrative deadlines (a right conditioned on age or a filing window) take
priority over any strategic consideration, every time. They live in `workspace/calendar.md`,
the single source of truth for dates, and get resolved before a broader conversation about
direction continues. See PATTE-CAR-06 in
[05-career-anti-patterns.md](05-career-anti-patterns.md).

## What a positioning review can't settle

The frameworks here, career capital, signaling, portfolio thinking, give structure, not an
answer to which specific lead to choose. That's deliberate: a sense of mission tends to emerge
from acting, not from settling everything in advance through deliberation. Leave what's still
genuinely open marked as open, rather than forcing a conclusion the evidence doesn't support
yet.
